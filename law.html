What we handle

The work behind every matter

Most of the strain in a firm is not in the legal work itself. It sits in everything around it, in the calls, the chasing, and the reading that has to happen before the real work can begin.

  1. 01

    The first response

    Someone calls after hours or reaches out in the language they are most comfortable in and needs a prompt, careful response, with the facts gathered and the next step booked before the moment passes.

  2. 02

    A file that keeps moving

    Records requested and followed up, deadlines watched, and clients kept informed, without everything depending on who remembered to check.

  3. 03

    Records you can actually use

    Medical records and billing turned into a clear chronology your team can read quickly, instead of pages someone has to work through before anyone can act.

  4. 04

    Drafts ready for judgment

    Early drafts that pull records, photos, and notes together, so an attorney can edit and decide instead of starting from a blank page.

  5. 05

    Files that answer back

  6. 06

    The quieter work

    Steady follow up with past clients, and content that brings in the kinds of matters you actually want, supported by training and plain language guidance the team can return to on an ordinary day.

Where the work waits

Where firms lose time and money

It is rarely one big problem. More often it is a few familiar places where work quietly waits, gets done twice, or depends entirely on memory.

Pattern 01

The inquiry that waited until morning

Someone calls in the evening, a form sits unread overnight, a callback happens a day later than it should. People who need help rarely wait for it, especially when another firm answers first.

Pattern 02

The follow up that lived in memory

A consultation never quite confirmed, records requested and never chased, a document a client promised that nobody went back for. Momentum is lost in the small gaps between one task and the next.

Pattern 03

Hours spent reading and reformatting

The facts are almost always in the file somewhere, buried in pages of records, billing, and notes. Careful preparation beforehand, with a person reviewing everything that matters, gives those hours back for the work that needs judgment.

Pattern 04

Happy clients who were never asked

The best matters still arrive by referral and reputation, yet asking for a review usually happens only when someone remembers in the moment. A steadier, more personal rhythm turns good endings into the next beginning.

A quick self check

If two or more sound familiar, a diagnostic is worth 30 minutes.

  • You are not quite sure what happens to a call that comes in late in the evening or over a weekend.
  • Getting back to people quickly is the intention, but it is hard to show that it happens every time.
  • Help in any language depends on who happens to be around that day.
  • Reading records and preparing drafts takes up hours that feel hard to spare each week.
  • You suspect there is a better way to handle some of this, but it is not clear where to begin.
  • People have tried a few tools on their own, and nothing quite became part of how the firm works.

Questions

Questions partners ask us

Partners ask careful questions before letting anyone near client work, and rightly so. Here are our answers, as plainly as we can give them.

Will this replace our case management system?

No. Your case system stays the source of truth, and everything we build logs back into it. We work alongside what your firm already runs, connecting through Zapier, Make, or n8n where that fits. If a workflow cannot connect cleanly, we will say so before anything is built, not after.

What about confidentiality and privilege?

Client work starts only inside tools your firm has chosen and approved, such as Claude Enterprise, ChatGPT Enterprise, or Gemini in your firm workspace, with access limited to the people who need it. No client files are placed into personal or consumer tools for convenience. And some workflows should not use AI at all. Part of the diagnostic is telling you plainly which ones those are at your firm.

Will AI speak to our clients without us knowing?

No. Everything begins in shadow mode, where the system drafts and logs and your team reviews before anything is sent. Client facing use, such as after hours intake coverage, happens only if you approve it after seeing it work, with full logs your team can read and a simple keyword that stops it at once.

Do we have to change our phone number or our systems to start?

No. Your main number is not ported and nothing is replaced to begin. We first map your current lead flow, so new support fits the way inquiries already arrive and nothing fires twice or falls between two systems.

Why bring in outside help for this at all?

Mostly because the everyday work does not pause while you figure out a new way of doing it. An outside perspective can map what is actually happening, make a clear plan, and get one useful thing working while your team keeps serving clients. Whatever is built is documented, so your firm can carry it forward in whatever way suits you best.

Which tools will you actually use?

Usually the ones your team already knows a little, such as Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini for reading and drafting, with Zapier, Make, and n8n connecting the systems around them and Relevance AI or Lindy where a small, focused helper makes sense. When an answer lives inside your documents, we draw it from your own files, and the accounts always stay in your firm name.

We tried AI tools before and staff did not use them.

That usually happens when a tool is placed on top of the work instead of inside it. We start with the workflow itself, build with the staff who will use it, trains them in person, and leaves written guidance behind. Adoption is part of the job, not an afterthought.

What does it cost?

The diagnostic itself is free. Afterwards the scope and price are put in writing before any build begins, based on what we found together and the one place it makes most sense to start. There is no standard price list because there is no standard problem, and the work is shaped around your firm rather than taken off a shelf.

Who owns the work if we stop working together?

You do. Accounts are opened in the firm name, workflows and documentation are handed over in full, and your team is trained to run them. We earn continued work by results, not by holding the keys.
