AI for manufacturing execution systems

MES data has structure. The operation gives it meaning.

Lots, routes, operations, equipment, holds, rework, recipes, and history tables can describe what happened. They do not automatically explain why it happened, which exceptions matter, or which answer the floor will trust.

Prime documents that context before AI is asked to reason over production data.

The trusted answer may not live in one place.

A production question can cross:

  • MES screens and reports;
  • underlying tables, views, procedures, and jobs;
  • equipment, quality, maintenance, planning, and ERP interfaces;
  • manual classifications and spreadsheet reconciliations;
  • rules and exceptions known by engineers, operators, and application owners.

The work is not merely to connect a model to the schema. It is to document which evidence matters and who can validate it.

Begin with one manufacturing decision.

  • How is the metric defined on the floor, in the report, and in the database?
  • Which lot, route, operation, resource, equipment, hold, or transaction records shape the answer?
  • Which relationships are documented and which are inferred?
  • Where do rework, overrides, late transactions, genealogy, or manual practices change interpretation?
  • Which report or example is trusted for validation?
  • Who owns the definition and who approves use?
  • What data should remain restricted?
  • What would an approved analysis or AI surface require?

A manufacturing context package your team can review.

  • system and interface inventory for the selected question;
  • priority entity and relationship map;
  • MES terminology and definition set;
  • calculation, state, exception, and rework register;
  • representative-example and validation plan;
  • ownership and restriction matrix;
  • readiness findings and next-step recommendation.

Approved and reviewable access.

Prime does not recommend unrestricted AI access to raw production tables as a default.

Where a later phase is appropriate, a proposed path may use client-approved read-only views, interfaces, or representative data; documented definitions and logic; named reviewers; and traceability appropriate to the use case.

The client’s technical, legal, privacy, security, compliance, data-owner, system-owner, and executive functions retain approval authority.

A strong fit has a system owner and a question that matters.

Examples include a disputed yield definition, a recurring WIP investigation, an equipment-state reporting problem, an unclear cycle-time calculation, or a proposed AI workflow whose answers operators do not yet trust.

Prime is not the right fit for bypassing MES controls, replacing subject-matter experts, or connecting a model directly to unrestricted production data.

Bring the manufacturing question, not a platform shopping list.
