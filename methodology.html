Our delivery framework

From operational ambiguity to a documented next step.

Prime starts with one operational question, then works through structure, meaning, ownership, controls, and validation before a larger technical commitment is made.

If the scope is not written, the work does not start.

See the four stages

A model can retrieve a value without understanding the operation.

The harder questions are not only where a value lives, but:

  • which calculation produced it;
  • which exception changes it;
  • which report people trust;
  • who can validate it;
  • which use is permitted;
  • what remains uncertain.

Prime makes that context reviewable so stakeholders can challenge the evidence, govern access, and decide the next step with clearer limits.

How the work is framed

Four stages. One written scope. No assumed package.

01 Discover

Clarify the decision, system, stakeholders, evidence, constraints, dependencies, and definition of useful.

02 Define

Document the technical structure and operational meaning behind the question: relationships, terms, calculations, transformations, exceptions, and disagreements.

03 Govern

Identify ownership, validation duties, proposed access paths, restrictions, logging or traceability needs, and decisions requiring client approval.

04 Activate

Define, and only when separately agreed implement, a pilot, approved data surface, workflow, analysis path, handoff, pause, or other next step.

Stages may overlap, shrink, repeat, or stop. Activities and timing depend on the environment and are confirmed in writing.

Inside the framework

Ten reference phases for planning the work.

These are planning lenses, not a ten-week promise and not a required sequence for every engagement.

01 Executive alignment

Define the operational decision, sponsor, boundaries, constraints, and success standard for the current phase.

Typical materials

problem statement, stakeholder and decision map, scope boundaries, phase success standard.

02 System and stakeholder inventory

Identify the applications, reports, owners, users, subject-matter experts, integrations, and dependencies that shape the question.

Typical materials

system inventory, stakeholder list, trusted-report shortlist, dependency notes.

03 Metadata and structure review

Examine approved schemas, tables, views, procedures, jobs, interfaces, reports, and available documentation.

Typical materials

object inventory, priority-object shortlist, initial classifications, documentation gaps.

04 Relationship and lineage analysis

Trace important entities, keys, transformations, flows, and dependencies, including inferred relationships.

Typical materials

relationship map with confidence labels, transformation notes, join warnings, open lineage questions.

05 Business meaning discovery

Document terminology, calculations, exceptions, accepted practices, ownership, and disagreements.

Typical materials

definition drafts, exception register, disagreement log, interview synthesis.

06 Validation and confidence review

Test findings against representative examples, trusted reports, and designated reviewers.

Typical materials

confidence-labeled findings, validation notes, unresolved-item list, readiness signals.

07 Access and governance design

Propose reviewable access paths, permissions, restrictions, validation gates, logging, and traceability for client consideration.

Typical materials

proposed access path, restriction notes, validation-gate outline, open risk list.

08 Pilot or workflow definition

Define the user, question, approved data surface, intended output, escalation path, and acceptance criteria for a possible next phase.

Typical materials

pilot charter, acceptance criteria, escalation path, go/no-go checklist.

09 Implementation or handoff

When separately scoped, create the agreed documentation, governed data surface, workflow, prototype, implementation plan, or handoff package.

Typical materials

agreed deliverables, maintenance notes, training or briefing materials, ownership summary.

10 Review and iteration

Compare what was learned with the phase success standard and recommend whether to expand, revise, pause, or stop.

Typical materials

review findings, lessons and open items, recommendation memo, optional next-scope outline.

A few rules that do not change.

Evidence over assumption

Confirmed facts, reasonable inferences, disagreements, unresolved questions, and restricted areas remain visibly separate.

Human expertise remains in the loop

Subject-matter experts validate definitions, exceptions, examples, and acceptable use.

Access is approved and reviewable

Prime does not recommend unrestricted AI access to raw operational tables as a default.

Client authority remains with the client

Legal, privacy, security, compliance, data-owner, system-owner, and executive decisions remain with the client’s designated functions.

Client-specific work is portable

Deliverables are provided under the ownership and licensing terms of the written agreement. Prime should not create unnecessary platform lock-in.

Stopping is a valid outcome

If evidence does not support a build, Prime says so.

A reviewable path, not a universal architecture.

An illustrative path may include:

  1. operational source systems;
  2. client-approved read-only or representative access;
  3. documented definitions and business logic;
  4. named validation and ownership;
  5. an approved analysis, AI, or analytics surface.

Any proposed architecture remains subject to client technical, legal, privacy, security, compliance, governance, and owner review.

Start with the system, the decision, and the people who understand it.
