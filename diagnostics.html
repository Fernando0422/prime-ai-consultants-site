Prime Diagnostics

Write the system down before you build on it.

One system. One operational domain. One decision. That boundary is intentional.

Prime Diagnostics documents the structure, meaning, ownership, validation, and access questions behind a proposed AI or analytics use case so your stakeholders can decide what should happen next.

Why it exists

A roadmap is not useful when no one agrees what the reports mean.

Most teams already know their data environment is complicated. What is often missing is a shared written view of:

  • which systems and reports matter;
  • how important metrics are calculated;
  • where exceptions and manual practices change the answer;
  • which relationships are documented and which are inferred;
  • who owns definitions and validation;
  • what access or use should remain restricted.

Prime Diagnostics converts those unknowns into evidence, findings, open questions, and decisions before a larger technical commitment is made.

The boundary

One system. One domain. One decision.

System

A defined MES, ERP, CRM, database, custom operational application, or closely related set of sources. Not every system in the enterprise.

Domain

A bounded operational area such as yield, quality, downtime, WIP, inventory, fulfillment, customer operations, or another clearly defined process.

Decision

A specific question, investigation, reporting disagreement, or proposed AI use case. If the decision cannot be stated, the engagement is not ready to begin.

Large environments may require several phases. A focused diagnostic is not an enterprise-wide data audit.

What we clarify

Questions we will not leave vague.

  1. Which systems, reports, interfaces, and data objects shape the selected decision?
  2. Where do the important relationships, calculations, transformations, and exceptions live?
  3. Which definitions are confirmed, inferred, disputed, or unresolved?
  4. Who owns the source, the definition, the validation, and the final decision?
  5. Which access paths appear appropriate for further evaluation?
  6. What should remain restricted pending legal, privacy, security, compliance, data-owner, or system-owner review?
  7. What evidence would be required to validate a future answer?
  8. What would a responsible next phase require, if any?

Possible outputs

Working materials your team can inspect and use.

System and stakeholder inventory

In-scope systems, databases, reports, interfaces, owners, users, and subject-matter experts tied to the selected question.

Priority relationship map

Important entities, flows, transformations, and boundaries, including relationships that are not represented by formal keys.

Operational data dictionary

Business meaning, source, ownership, sensitivity, relationships, confidence, and open questions for priority objects and fields.

Business-logic and exception register

Calculations, report rules, stored logic, application behavior, overrides, exceptions, and expert practices discovered during the work.

Validation and ownership matrix

Who can confirm each definition, calculation, example, or exception, and which items remain unresolved.

Readiness findings

Access needs, restrictions, assumptions, risks, dependencies, and decisions that require an owner.

Recommended next-step scope

What to resolve first, what may be ready for a pilot or implementation, and what should wait.

Not every engagement requires every output. The final scope, activities, outputs, timing, responsibilities, and fees are confirmed in writing.

Working process

A focused engagement, shaped around evidence.

01 Align

Confirm the decision, system boundary, stakeholders, constraints, available evidence, and standard for a useful result.

02 Examine

Review client-approved metadata, documentation, reports, logic, relationships, and representative examples.

03 Interpret

Compare the technical structure with how operators, analysts, engineers, and system owners describe the process.

04 Validate

Label findings as confirmed, inferred, disputed, unresolved, or restricted. Assign owners to the questions that remain open.

05 Recommend

Deliver the working materials, explain limitations, and recommend a practical next step. The recommendation may be to pause.

Timing depends on scope, access, evidence, stakeholder availability, and review cycles. It is confirmed only in the written agreement.

The work depends on access to people and evidence.

  • an executive or operational sponsor;
  • a named system or data owner;
  • relevant subject-matter experts;
  • client-approved access to documentation, metadata, reports, or representative data;
  • examples of trusted and disputed outputs;
  • participation from legal, privacy, security, compliance, or other reviewers when the scope requires it;
  • timely decisions on open questions and access requests.

Prime does not bypass client controls or assume access.

Who this is for, and who it is not.

Strong fit

A meaningful operational question, a defined system, an identified owner, available experts, and a willingness to document uncertainty before building.

Not a fit

A generic chatbot, a consumer application, unrestricted access to production tables, a rapid demo with no validation path, or work with no system owner and no operational question.
