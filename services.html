Three ways to engage.

Prime Diagnostics

Discover and define

Document one system, one domain, and one decision. Clarify the structure, meaning, business logic, ownership, validation duties, restrictions, and open questions that shape the use case.

Possible outputs

  • system and stakeholder inventory;
  • priority relationship map;
  • operational data dictionary;
  • business-logic and exception register;
  • validation and ownership matrix;
  • readiness findings;
  • recommended next-step scope.

Best when

the team does not yet share a written view of the system or decision.

Explore Prime Diagnostics

Prime Build

Design and implement

When discovery supports a build, Prime can help define and implement the approved data surface, documentation, workflow, prototype, or governed analysis path described in a separate written scope.

Possible outputs

  • architecture and data-surface design;
  • approved read-only views or interfaces;
  • documented definitions and calculation logic;
  • validation and escalation workflow;
  • query, access, or decision traceability appropriate to the scope;
  • implementation documentation and handoff materials;
  • owner briefing or training.

Best when

the system, decision, owners, validation path, access constraints, and acceptance criteria are sufficiently understood.

Discuss a build

Prime Retainer

Maintain and extend

Ongoing support for client-approved materials, definitions, views, workflows, and governance practices as systems and operations change.

Possible activities

  • definition and documentation updates;
  • new or revised approved data surfaces;
  • periodic review of assumptions, restrictions, and open questions;
  • performance or maintainability review;
  • stakeholder briefings;
  • planning for additional use cases.

Best when

the client has a live body of work that needs named ownership and controlled evolution.

Discuss ongoing support

Final scope, activities, outputs, timing, responsibilities, ownership, licensing, and fees are defined in a written agreement.

Vendor-neutral by design.

Prime begins with the client’s actual environment. A platform name does not establish business meaning, data quality, permitted use, or readiness.

References to third-party products describe environments that may be relevant to the method. They do not imply partnership, certification, sponsorship, endorsement, or a claim of implementation experience unless expressly stated and supported.

Clear boundaries before work begins.

  • a written purpose and scope;
  • named client stakeholders and decision owners;
  • approved access and information-handling expectations;
  • defined activities and possible outputs;
  • assumptions, dependencies, and exclusions;
  • review and acceptance responsibilities;
  • commercial and legal terms in a separate agreement.
