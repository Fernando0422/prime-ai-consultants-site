What usually breaks first

The model is rarely the first problem.

The data often exists. Shared meaning does not.

Operations trusts the application screen. Finance trusts an export. Analysts maintain spreadsheets to reconcile the two. Critical exceptions live with people who have spent years inside the process.

When that context is missing, AI makes disagreement faster. It does not make the answer trustworthy.

Card 1: Different sources

The application, report, export, and database may each present a different version of the same event.

Card 2: Different definitions

Terms such as yield, downtime, backlog, qualified opportunity, or available inventory can have several defensible meanings.

Card 3: Unwritten logic

Calculations, overrides, relationships, and operating practices often live outside the schema.

Card 4: Unclear ownership

If no one owns validation, a plausible answer can circulate without anyone being accountable for it.

Where most teams start

One system. One domain. One decision.

Prime Diagnostics is deliberately bounded. It is not an enterprise-wide data audit and it is not a promise to implement AI.

It is a focused engagement designed to answer:

  • Which systems, reports, and data objects matter for the selected decision?
  • Where do important relationships, calculations, and exceptions live?
  • Which definitions are confirmed, inferred, disputed, or unresolved?
  • Who owns validation and approval?
  • What should remain restricted pending legal, privacy, security, compliance, or owner review?
  • What would a credible next phase require?

Possible outputs

  • system and stakeholder inventory;
  • priority relationship map;
  • operational data dictionary;
  • business-logic and exception register;
  • validation and ownership matrix;
  • readiness findings and open decisions;
  • recommended next-step scope.

Final activities, outputs, timing, and fees are confirmed in a written agreement.

Who this is for

A useful fit starts with a real system and a real question.

Strong fit

  • An MES, ERP, CRM, database, report environment, or custom operational application is central to the question.
  • Teams disagree about definitions, calculations, sources, or ownership.
  • A system owner and relevant subject-matter experts can participate.
  • The organization is willing to examine context and access before building.
  • Stakeholders want documented findings and clear limits, not a demo at any cost.

Probably not a fit

  • A generic marketing chatbot.
  • A consumer application unrelated to operational systems.
  • A rapid prototype with no system owner, validation path, or access review.
  • A request to connect AI directly to unrestricted production tables.
