What's usually wrong

The model isn't the first problem.

The data's usually there. Meaning isn't. Reports don't agree. Application logic has no owner. Spreadsheets fill the gaps. And half the real knowledge lives in someone who's been on the floor for twenty years.

  1. 01

    Different sources

    Ops trusts the MES screen. Finance trusts last night's export. Nobody designed them to agree.

  2. 02

    Different meanings

    Ask how yield is calculated. You'll get two answers. Maybe three. Both can sound right. Neither is written down.

  3. 03

    Different answers

    Skip the writing and AI just makes the confusion faster. That's it.

The job is to make meaning visible across the layers that matter for the decision you're actually trying to answer.

How the weeks usually go

Messy operations in. A documented next step out. Scoped in writing. Never assumed.

  1. 01

    Discover

    What's the system? What's the decision? Who owns it? What evidence already exists? Reports that don't match, interfaces, tribal knowledge, the places meaning already breaks. All of that counts.

  2. 02

    Define

    Relationships, calculations, terminology, the exceptions everyone already knows. We dig those out so the use case matches how the floor runs, not how the schema looks on paper.

  3. 03

    Govern

    Access paths, who owns validation, what's restricted, what's still open. We write it up. Your technical, security, and governance people get to poke holes in it.

  4. 04

    Activate

    Only then do we shape a pilot, data surface, analysis workflow, or next investment. Understanding first. Implementation after. If that order flips, we push back.

Activities, timing, and outputs vary. A lot.

After a first conversation, we recommend only the stage that fits. Then it goes in a written scope.

Where most people start

One system. One domain. One decision.

Prime Diagnostics is short on purpose. Pick one system and one operational question. We also write down ownership, validation, and access issues that need review before anyone talks about building.

What we're trying to settle

  • Which systems and objects actually matter for this question
  • Where the relationships, calculations, and exceptions hide
  • Which definitions are settled, and which still need an owner
  • What only lives in someone's head
  • What a responsible next phase would actually require

Possible outputs

System inventory
In-scope systems, reports, interfaces, and known owners tied to the selected question.
Priority relationship map
How the important entities, data flows, and boundaries actually connect.
Business-logic register
Calculations, report rules, application quirks, and the expert practices we dug up during the work.
Readiness findings
Access, ownership, validation, restrictions, assumptions, and the next decisions still sitting open.

Final scope, activities, outputs, and timing get confirmed in a written agreement.
