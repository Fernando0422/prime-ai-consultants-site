AI for CRM

Customer data needs context before automation touches it.

CRM environments mix customer records, sales activity, service history, workflow rules, notes, integrations, and sensitive fields. Before an AI or analytics initiative proceeds, someone has to define the system, the question, the ownership, and the controls that need review. That's the work we do. Selectively.

Our stance

We're selective about CRM work. On purpose.

Our strongest experience is manufacturing and enterprise applications. CRM work is selective. We take it on when the problem depends on complex operational context, and when the expertise, access, and client governance functions are actually available.

We won't pretend we're a full-stack CRM shop. If the work needs a specialist platform partner more than a context investigation, we'll say so.

  • Decision first A named workflow or business question, not an open-ended CRM cleanup
  • Sensitive fields matter Personal, confidential, or regulated data needs named owners before access expands
  • Humans stay in review Validation and escalation remain part of the workflow when answers affect customers

Questions we won't leave fuzzy

What has to be clear before automation proceeds

  • What business decision or workflow is actually under consideration?
  • Which data is necessary for that purpose, and which isn't?
  • Which fields contain personal, sensitive, confidential, or regulated information?
  • Who owns the definitions and access decisions?
  • What human review has to stay in the workflow?
  • Which uses should not proceed without additional legal, privacy, security, compliance, or customer review?

We do not provide privacy, employment, consumer-protection, credit, healthcare, or sector-specific legal advice. CRM use cases involving personal information require review by the client's qualified legal, privacy, security, compliance, and data-owner functions.

How we approach it

Write the decision, the data, and the accountability first.

If those aren't clear enough to put in writing, we don't start. Selectivity is a feature, not a gap in the brochure.

  1. Name the decision

    Confirm the workflow or question, the systems involved, and what "useful" would look like.

  2. Map necessary data

    Separate fields required for the purpose from fields that should stay out of scope.

  3. Assign ownership

    Write down who owns definitions, access, validation, and escalation before anything connects.

  4. Decide whether to proceed

    Recommend a next step only when the context and client review path are written.
